Method

How a report is made.

Each step, in order, and what the result can and cannot tell you.

1. The briefs

A report uses three buyer briefs. Each one describes a realistic buyer in your market: company size, the job to be done, the systems it must connect to, budget and security needs. Each brief ends in a fixed set of questions, usually six. We write the briefs with you and you approve them before anything runs.

2. The sites

Each brief is run against your website and two competitors you choose. The assistant is told to use each vendor's own public website and to name the page behind every answer.

3. The assistants

We use real, publicly available AI assistants with web access, the same ones buyers use. The report names each assistant and the date of every run.

4. Grading each answer

Every answer gets one of three statuses:

  • Verified The assistant answered and named a page, and that page says what the assistant claimed.
  • Partly answered Part of the question is answered on the site; part is missing or vague.
  • Not found The assistant could not find it, or its answer is not supported by the page it named.

A person checks every cited page. We record the assistant's overall verdict for each vendor (shortlisted, maybe, or not shortlisted) as it gave it.

5. The fix list

Gaps are ranked by how often they appear across the briefs and whether a competitor answered the same question. Each fix names the fact, the page where it belongs, and suggested wording for you to edit.

What we read, and what we never do

  • Public pages only, fetched at a polite rate, obeying robots.txt.
  • No logins, no forms submitted, no contact with your team or customers.
  • No tag on your site and no visitor data. We identify nobody.

Limits

  • A report is a snapshot of specific assistants on a specific day. Assistants change often.
  • The same question can get a different answer on another run. We say how many runs a report used.
  • Assistants also draw on what they learned in training and on third-party sites. A report measures what your own site supports, which is the part you control.
  • Three briefs do not cover every buyer. They cover the ones you agreed matter most.
  • Our study figures on the home page come from 15 vendors, one assistant and one run each. They illustrate the problem; they are not a benchmark.
